5 Business Tasks to Review for AI Automation

Before choosing another AI tool, look at the work your business repeats every day. Some tasks may benefit from AI assistance—but the right starting point is a clear process, approved information, a person responsible for review and a way to measure whether the change helps.

Practical guideSmall businessAI automationReviewed 16 Nov 2026

Important: These are workflow ideas to assess, not a recommendation to automate every task. Suitability depends on how often the task occurs, the quality and sensitivity of its inputs, the consequences of an error and whether a person can verify the output.

1. Sorting customer enquiries

Where the manual effort appears: Requests arrive through a website form or shared inbox. Someone reads each message, identifies its topic and decides who should handle it.

Possible AI-assisted flow:

  1. Receive an enquiry through an approved channel.
  2. Classify it using a small, predefined set of categories.
  3. Extract basic details such as the requested service and enquiry type.
  4. Prepare a short summary or routing suggestion.
  5. Ask a team member to confirm the category and handle exceptions.

Keep a person responsible for: complaints, unusual requests, sensitive matters and any customer commitment.

Measure: time to first review, classification corrections and the number of enquiries requiring manual rerouting.

2. Drafting routine replies

Where the manual effort appears: Staff repeatedly answer common questions about services, opening hours, appointment requirements or next steps.

Possible AI-assisted flow:

  1. Create an approved source of FAQs, service details and policies.
  2. Generate a draft using only that approved information.
  3. Flag missing information instead of inventing an answer.
  4. Have a staff member review and send the message.

Keep a person responsible for: prices, refunds, complaints, commitments and sensitive customer situations.

Measure: draft preparation time, correction rate and the number of questions left unanswered.

3. Extracting information from documents and forms

Where the manual effort appears: Someone reads routine documents and copies selected fields into a spreadsheet or business system.

Possible AI-assisted flow:

  1. Use only documents the business is authorized to process.
  2. Extract a limited set of clearly named fields.
  3. Mark missing or uncertain values.
  4. Compare extracted values with the original document.
  5. Approve the record before it is used in another process.

Keep a person responsible for: checking names, dates, amounts, identifiers and any field that affects a customer, payment or official record.

Measure: time per record, field-level error rate and rework.

4. Preparing meeting and call summaries

Where the manual effort appears: Team members turn notes or approved recordings into summaries, decisions and follow-up tasks.

Possible AI-assisted flow:

  1. Use notes or recordings only where appropriate notice and permission are in place.
  2. Draft a summary, decisions and proposed action items.
  3. Link actions to source notes where possible.
  4. Ask a participant to verify names, decisions, owners and due dates.

Keep a person responsible for: confirming the summary before it becomes the official record.

Measure: preparation time, corrections required and completion of confirmed actions.

5. Creating weekly operations reports

Where the manual effort appears: A team collects figures from approved spreadsheets or systems and prepares a recurring internal report.

Possible AI-assisted flow:

  1. Use only authorized and relevant data.
  2. Calculate totals with validated formulas or trusted system outputs.
  3. Use AI to draft a plain-language explanation from verified figures.
  4. Compare every reported figure with its source before sharing.

Keep a person responsible for: validating calculations, definitions, date ranges and business context. An AI-generated explanation is not proof of why a metric changed.

Measure: report preparation time, numerical error rate and usefulness to the decision-maker.

How to choose a suitable first task

Review each candidate against these five questions. A score from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) can help compare options, but it is only a screening aid—not permission to automate a sensitive decision.

  • Repeatability: does the same task happen regularly?
  • Clear inputs: are the required inputs known and available?
  • Checkable output: can a person verify the result against a reliable source?
  • Manageable risk: would an error be easy to detect and correct before harm occurs?
  • Measurable value: can the business measure time, quality or rework?
Do not start with high-impact decisions. Tasks affecting a person's safety, rights, employment, eligibility, credit, legal position or major financial commitments need stronger controls and qualified oversight. Avoid giving an AI system independent authority over such decisions.

Run a small, controlled pilot

  1. Choose one narrow workflow that happens often.
  2. Write down how it works today, including time spent and common errors.
  3. Identify the data involved and confirm that its use in the selected tool is authorized.
  4. Test a small sample and define what a correct output looks like.
  5. Assign a person to review every output during the pilot.
  6. Record errors, corrections, exceptions and time spent.
  7. Compare the pilot with the original process.
  8. Stop, revise or expand only when the evidence supports the decision.

Do not upload confidential client records, personal information or proprietary data into an AI tool until the business has checked permissions, vendor terms, retention and security settings.

What should you ask before automating?

  • Which task is repeated?
  • What makes it slow or inconsistent?
  • What information is needed—and is it safe to use?
  • What could go wrong?
  • Who checks the output?
  • How will improvement be measured?

The right solution may be a simple rule-based workflow, an AI-assisted step or no automation at all. Choose the least complex approach that solves the actual problem.
